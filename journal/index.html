Journal 1: Research Form Design

1/17/2026

In the article which covers best practices about forms, forms want to be super straightforward and simple to understand, because people of all abilities and ages are going to have to fill out forms. You should follow the “less is more” idea. One single column should be used so the user doesn’t have to return back up the page. Or, if there is a lot of content needed to fill out on the form, you can create multiple steps with a next button. This should have a number of steps like or percentage of completion with it. If something requires special formatting, you put rules below it to inform the user. A label is best positioned above the input field, or for larger screens, you can put it to the left. The labels should always be placed near the input field in proximity to show relation. You shouldn’t put the label inside the box because users can forget what they are supposed to input. Same thing with putting examples, sometimes they can be in the input field, but for accessibility they can also be put below. Input fields should be the length that the question is asking. If you can, minimize dropdown menus and use radio buttons instead. These are all things I’ve never really considered that have to be thought about when designing forms, but I guess you know you’ve come across a bad form when you have difficulty navigating the page or knowing exactly what you’re supposed to fill out in a form. I liked that the article included the point about when forms ask for sensitive information. It’s nice when important numbers like SSN’s are replaced with asterisks or the form explains why exactly they need certain information from you. The action button is important as well as it needs to be worded correctly. Providing user feedback is important as well. I thought Duolingo’s beginning sign up form is really effective because it goes through the questions one question at a time and has their mascot, the green owl, interact with the user’s input and give immediate feedback. Before asking you to create an account, they delve into your current skills of speaking/writing a language which shows they prioritize the information regarding the purpose of the app first.
